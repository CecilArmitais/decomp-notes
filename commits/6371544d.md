## Decompiled

Both from `asm/main_02056960.s`, into a new `src/main_02056D70.c`.

**Fact.** Each walks the four entries of `TEAM_MEMBER_TABLE_PTR->active_roster`
and, for every entry with bit 1 of its flags byte set, takes `member_index` and
keeps it if `IsMonsterIdInNormalRange` accepts it and a filter rejects it:
`GetUnitNpcIds` excludes the three main characters via `sub_02055410` /
`sub_02055474` / `sub_020554D8`; `GetAdventureNpcIds` excludes via
`sub_020564B0`. Matching indices are appended to the caller's buffer and the
count returned.

## The null-buffer stack frame

**Fact.** Both tolerate a null buffer. The target reserves **eight bytes of
stack** and redirects the pointer there when the argument is null -- which is why
the frame reserves space nothing else uses. Reproduced with a local
`s16 scratch[4]`.

This is a good example of a frame oddity that is *meaningful*, not noise: an
unexplained stack reservation is often an out-parameter fallback.

## The bitfield that cannot be used

**Fact, and worth reading carefully.** The flag test reads the first byte of
`struct team_member` **directly**, as `(*(u8 *)m & 2)`.

`common.h` models that byte as a bitfield -- `f_is_valid:1` followed by
`flags_unk1:7` -- and **no access through those members reproduces the target's
single `tst`**:

- reading `flags_unk1` emits a shift pair to extract the field first;
- splitting bit 1 into its own one-bit member is worse still.

The raw byte read is the only form found that matches, so it is used here rather
than changing a struct shared with the rest of the tree.

**This is a trade a reviewer should weigh.** The C is less expressive than the
struct allows, in exchange for not perturbing a shared type. The alternative --
restructuring `struct team_member`'s bitfields -- was rejected as too invasive
for this commit, not as impossible.

## A tightening that rippled

**Fact.** The buffer parameter is `s16*` -- what both call sites pass and what
the stores write. The prototypes move out of `main_02064FFC.h`, where they were
declared as `void*`, into the new header.

That tightening **forced** `sub_02065050`'s two scratch buffers from `u8[8]` to
`s16[4]` -- the same eight bytes, and already how `sub_0206549C` declares its
own. `sub_02065050` was rebuilt to confirm byte-identity.

A concrete instance of the general rule: **the real signature is usually
narrower than the guess made from a call site**, and narrowing it changes the
caller.
