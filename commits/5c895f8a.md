## Building a faithful context, completely

**Fact.** The context is now the real headers: `mwccarm -E` over a file including
`global.pch`, `dungeon.h` and `mission.h`, yielding ~14,500 lines that reproduce
what the build sees.

Three things it needs, none of them obvious, all of which cost a round trip:

1. **`LM_LICENSE_FILE` must be set** or `-E` fails a license check before doing
   anything.
2. **`global.pch` must be included first.** Without it the output dies at
   `typedef s32 fx32_8;` in `util.h`, because `s32` comes from `<nitro.h>`, which
   the build supplies through `-prefix global.mch` — not through any `#include`
   in the header being preprocessed.
3. **`-E` consumes every macro.** `TRUE`, `FALSE` and `NULL` are simply gone and
   must be pasted back from `lib/include/nitro/types.h` (`NULL` is
   `((void *)0)` on the C branch). This one is easy to misdiagnose: the failure
   appears as `undefined identifier 'FALSE'` **in the candidate**, which reads
   like a mistake in the C.

**The check that makes a new oracle trustworthy:** re-score functions that are
already landed and matching, and require all of them to come back 0. Eight were
available here and all did. Without that step there is no reason to believe the
second context any more than the first.

## What changed under the new oracle, and what did not

**Fact.** Of the thirteen that had stalled:

- **Eight score 0 unchanged** and are landed here exactly as previously written.
  Their earlier scores happened to be right; the point is that they were not
  *evidence* until now.
- **`GetMissionEnemyMinionGroup` does not.** The real headers let the compiler
  fold the `+1` of `enemy_species[index + 1]` into the load displacement, one
  instruction shorter than the target. **Binding `index + 1` to a local first
  blocks the fold** and scores 0:

  ```c
  s32 i = index + 1;

  return DUNGEON_PTR->mission_destination.enemy_species[i];
  ```

  Two spellings that do **not** work: taking `enemy_species` as a pointer, or
  taking `&enemy_species[1]` as a pointer — both still fold, at 110.

- **Four are genuinely unmatched**, and were never mis-measured:
  `IsCurrentMissionType` and `IsCurrentMissionTypeExact` at **515**,
  `GetItemToRetrieve` at **665**, `GetItemToDeliver` at **1530**. **Identical
  scores under both contexts**, which is itself informative — it says the context
  was never their problem.

## The enumerators

**Fact.** The `mission_type` arguments use `MISSION_ARREST_OUTLAW`,
`MISSION_EXPLORE_WITH_CLIENT`, `MISSION_TAKE_ITEM_FROM_OUTLAW` and
`MISSION_CHALLENGE_REQUEST` from `mission.h`, because the build rejects the
implicit `int` conversion the scratch accepted.

**Worth noting, not claiming:** the values agree with the function names —
`IsOutlawMonsterHouseFloor` uses `ARREST_OUTLAW`, `IsDestinationFloorWithHiddenOutlaw`
uses `TAKE_ITEM_FROM_OUTLAW`, `IsJirachiChallengeFloor` uses `CHALLENGE_REQUEST`.
They were read off the numbers, not chosen to fit the names.

## Open questions

- **`IsCurrentMissionType` / `IsCurrentMissionTypeExact` (515).** The target
  branches to two separate `mov`/`bx` tails; every spelling tried produces
  predicated moves or the wrong tail order. Tried: `&&` in one `if`, nested
  `if`s, guard-style early returns with the second test inverted. Untried: a
  result local assigned in both paths, and whether the original shared a helper.
- **`GetItemToRetrieve` (665) / `GetItemToDeliver` (1530).** A pointer-local form
  brings the first to 200. The target mixes a `dungeon`-based load for the flag
  with a `mission_destination`-based load for the item, which no single spelling
  has reproduced.
- **13 of 21 landed** across three commits; 4 unmatched, and 4 more
  (`CheckActiveChallengeRequest` and neighbours) never attempted.
