## The funnel shape generalises

**Fact.** All three move effects share the shape established by `DoMoveBubble`
in [`78b52f9d`](78b52f9d.md): deal damage, and on a random outcome apply a
secondary effect — returning **whether damage landed**, not whether the effect
did. Once that shape was known all three matched on the first candidate, which
is the first time a shape found in one sweep paid off directly in the next.

**Inference.** The result variable doubles as an argument in two of them:
`DoMoveMistBall` passes it as both `LowerOffensiveStat`'s stage count and its
`checkProtected` flag, and `DoMoveMudSlap` passes a literal `0` as the random
chance. A zero chance is odd on its face; nothing here explains it, and it is
recorded as read from the bytes rather than rationalised.

**Fact.** `GetEquivItemIndex` walks 50 bag slots with a cursor and tests the
exists flag as `(bag->flags & 1) != 0` — the boolify spelling from
`MATCHING_TIPS.md`, needed here to get `tst`/`movne`/`moveq` rather than a bare
`and`.

## The fifth, not landed

**`GetNextIqSkill`** (`asm/main_02058F24.s`) stands at **30**. The candidate is
otherwise instruction-for-instruction correct; the entire difference is that the
target keeps its skill-list cursor in **`lr`** while the candidate uses `r5`.
Both functions push the same register set, so this is a colouring choice, not a
pressure difference.

**Falsified** (all 30 or worse): hoisting the `value` local out of the loop
(55), moving the cursor's declaration to the end of the block (785), computing
the IQ group into a temporary first (30), and rewriting the `for` as a `while`
(30).

The reconstructed logic — walk up to 25 skill ids, stop at `0xFF`, skip `0`, and
keep the smallest `IQ_SKILLS[skill]` strictly greater than the argument — is
believed right precisely because everything except the register matches.
