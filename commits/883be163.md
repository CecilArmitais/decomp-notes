## What this fixes

[`e8823a88`](e8823a88.md) landed `SetLeaderAction` against the US target only,
and claimed JP was expected not to match while EU was unaffected. **Both claims
were wrong.** EU was broken outright, and JP needed one guarded call rather than
the rewrite the note predicted.

**Fact.** Baselines were measured before changing anything: EU and JP both
matched at `e8823a88~1`, so the previous commit regressed two working targets.

## The three constant sites are immediate encodability, not source

**Fact, arithmetic.** The message ids differ by `SET_LEADER_ACTION_DATA_OFFSET`
= `-0x2C1` on JP. The pair used at those two arms is `0xBA0` / `0xBA1` on US,
which is `0x8DF` / `0x8E0` on JP.

**Fact, checked against the ARM encoding rules.** `0xBA0` and `0x8E0` are
representable as a single rotated 8-bit immediate; `0xBA1` and `0x8DF` are not.

**Inference, but forced by the above.** In each region MWCC materializes the
encodable one inline with `mov` and derives the other from the pooled
`0xBA3 + OFFSET`, caching it in a stack slot. The offset shift swaps which of
the pair is encodable, so the two arms appear to trade places between regions.
The C is identical; only the codegen differs. This is why JP needed no
conditional here, and it is the part the previous note got most wrong.

## The two real differences

**Fact.** `SetDecoyAiTracker(GetLeader())` is US/EU only. Its declaration in
`include/overlay_29_022FB538.h` is *already* inside `#ifndef JAPAN`, so calling
it unconditionally failed the JP build at **compile time**, not at the checksum.
Upstream had encoded the region fact; the previous commit ignored it.

**Fact.** `ov29_022F2FE4()` is an extra call EU makes immediately after
`ov29_022E0B44()` in the tail block. It comes from an `#ifdef EUROPE` in the
asm. Nothing declares this function anywhere in the tree, so it keeps a local
`extern` in this file.

## How the error happened, and the check that would have caught it

The previous commit's region survey grepped the replaced asm for `JAPAN` and
never for `EUROPE`, so the EU-only call was invisible. A conditional-site survey
should enumerate **every** preprocessor directive in the block
(`grep -n '^#'`), not the ones for the region being reasoned about.

Corroboration that cost nothing once the sites were listed: preprocessing the
block per region gives EU = US + 1 instruction and JP = US - 2, which is exactly
one added call and one removed two-call block.
