## Decompiled

`GetHero`, `GetPartner`, `GetMainCharacter1`, `GetMainCharacter2`,
`GetMainCharacter3` -- all from `asm/main_02054E4C.s`, merging into a new
`src/main_02055770.c`.

**Fact.** Each returns a pointer to a `struct ground_monster` in
`TEAM_MEMBER_TABLE_PTR->members`, or `NULL` when that entry's `is_valid` bit is
clear. `GetHero` returns `members[0]`, `GetPartner` `members[1]`; the
`GetMainCharacter` functions pick the index from the game mode --
`members[2]/[3]/[4]` during a special episode, `members[0]/[1]`/`NULL`
otherwise.

## Why this commit matters beyond five bodies

**It confirms by construction what three earlier commits asserted.** The
accessors really do return `struct ground_monster*`, so the `->id` reads feeding
the monster-id output of `sub_0206549C` are reading `struct ground_monster::id`
-- the documented "Monster ID" field.

That is the payoff of the callee-before-caller ordering: a claim made from a call
site in [`39a8db1b`](39a8db1b.md) becomes a fact once the callee's body exists.

## Prototype lifecycle

**Fact.** These prototypes were temporarily declared in `main_02064FFC.h` while
the earlier commits needed them. They move to the new `main_02055770.h`, which
`main_02064FFC.h` now includes. `sub_02056914` keeps its prototype in the old
header because it is still asm.

This is the intended lifecycle for a provisional declaration, executed properly:
declare weakly at the caller, then **move and delete** when the callee lands.
