## Two identical bodies, kept identical

**Fact.** `SetBpm` and `SetBpm2` have **identical bodies**. The two differ only
in the label their literal pool uses.

**Inference, and the reason for the choice.** They are the same routine reached
from two opcodes rather than variants of one. Both are decompiled **as written**
-- rather than one calling the other -- because that is what the target does.

Worth stating: the temptation to "de-duplicate" here would produce different
bytes and a wrong decompilation.

## The constant, decoded

**Fact.** They scale the sequence's tempo fade by the new bpm and divide
`0x03938700` by the result to get `microseconds_per_beat`, guarding a zero
divisor by substituting 1.

**Fact.** `0x03938700` is 60,000,000 -- sixty million microseconds, i.e. one
minute. Decoding it is what makes the expression legible.

**Fact.** The shifts type the intermediate: the tempo is read with an
**arithmetic** shift and the product with a **logical** one, so the value handed
to `_u32_div_f` is unsigned.

## A signature taken from a type, not a call site

**Fact.** `Signal` writes the stream byte to the sequence and passes it to the
sequence's `signal_callback` with code 8, following
`DseTrackEvent_SetInstrument` in `lib/DSE/src/main_02071BF4.c`, which makes the
same call with a different code.

**Fact, and the better source of evidence.** The callback's four arguments --
`id`, `code`, `value`, `callback_arg` -- come from the **existing
function-pointer type in `dse.h`**, not from the call site. A declared type in a
canonical header beats an inference from one invocation.
