**Fact.** It picks one of 18 title-screen demo teams at random and stores that
team's hero and partner species into the `SPECIAL_ACTORS` demo slots.

## Evidence for the table layout

**Fact, read off the asm.** `DEMO_TEAMS` is an array of a 4-byte record, 18
entries, two 16-bit fields read via `ldrsh`. The stride comes from the index
scaling, the field offsets from the load displacements, and the signedness from
`ldrsh` rather than `ldrh`.

**Inference, deliberately unasserted.** What those two fields *mean*. They keep
placeholder names -- `struct unk_020A68C8`, `field_0x0`, `field_0x2` -- because
identifying this table was not part of the change. `DEMO_TEAMS` itself already
existed in the tree and keeps its name.

**Fact worth knowing when diffing against the asm.** A separate `.global`,
`_020A68CA`, sits at `DEMO_TEAMS + 2` -- on the second field of the *first*
record. Both symbols resolve to the same address.

That aliasing is not a curiosity: it recurs across this whole branch, and it is
what decides which symbol appears in a literal pool. See
[`7f6977e2`](7f6977e2.md), where getting it wrong cost a build.

## The signed-enum question, first appearance

**Fact.** The species are cast to `enum monster_id` when stored. Under
`-enum min` an enum with no negative enumerator is **unsigned**, so it cannot
reproduce a signed (`ldrsh`) read.

**Left open here, deliberately.** Whether `monster_id` should declare a `-1`
sentinel -- the game *does* return `-1` from `GetScriptEntityMonsterId` -- was
left for upstream discussion rather than diverging from pmdsky-debug.

**The branch later reversed this.** [`39a8db1b`](39a8db1b.md) adds
`MONSTER_INVALID = -1` on the evidence of nine `ldrsh` reads. A reviewer reading
these in order should see a position that changed under evidence, and is free to
disagree with where it landed.
