Five functions, 106 instructions: `sub_02050EBC` (21), `SubFixedPoint` (26),
`sub_02050F78` (32), `sub_02050FF8` (10) and `BinToDecFixedPoint` (17). They are
the last five `arm_func` blocks in `asm/main_020504BC.s` and all operate on
`struct fixed_point`.

## Why five and not three

They were taken as step one toward `ActivateEndOfTurnEffects`
([`a562678d`](a562678d.md)), which calls three of them and whose by-value
`struct fixed_point` ABI was the one structural unknown in its census. Two facts
turned three into five:

- **Fact.** The cluster is contiguous and sits at the end of the file.
- **Fact.** `SubFixedPoint` cannot be landed alone as a merge.
  `extract_function.py` merges only a function at the *start or end* of its
  `.s`; `sub_02050F78` sits between `SubFixedPoint` and the end, so landing only
  the three would split the object or leave the cluster interleaved.

## Landing shape

**Fact**, read off `tools/extract_function/extract_function.py` (lines 116-146,
251-268) and confirmed by the run: `main.lsf:275-276` puts
`asm/main_020504BC.o` immediately above `src/number_util.o`, and a **last**
function in a `.s` sets no new-file address, so the tool merges into the next
`src/` object and **`main.lsf` is not touched**. `asm/main_020504BC.s` keeps its
other 28 functions.

Landed in **reverse address order** so each prepends above the previous, leaving
`src/number_util.c` in ascending address order with `CeilFixedPoint` last.
Landing forward would have reversed them.

## Naming

`sub_02050EBC` and `sub_02050F78` **keep their `sub_<addr>` names**. The task was
decompilation, not identification, and a match proves bytes, never meaning.
Locals and parameters are generic.

Reading the bodies, `sub_02050EBC` is the addition counterpart of
`SubFixedPoint` and `sub_02050F78` returns the smaller of its two arguments —
but that is **inference from the arithmetic**, offered only so a reviewer who
wants to name them upstream has somewhere to start.

## The ABI

**Fact**, established by `sub_02050FF8` reaching score 0 on its first candidate
rather than assumed:

- a 4-byte `struct fixed_point` is passed and returned in `r0`, packed as
  `orr r0, r0, r1, lsl #16` (integer low, fractional high);
- `stmdb sp!, {r0, r1, r2, r3}` at entry is MWCC giving the incoming argument
  registers stack homes so a by-value struct parameter can be addressed, and
  writing to the parameter reuses that home — which is why `SubFixedPoint` and
  `sub_02050EBC` modify their first argument and return it.

**The caller side needed nothing.** The census of `ActivateEndOfTurnEffects` had
flagged the `sub rX, sp, #4 / strh / strh / ldr` sequence around every by-value
call as an unknown construct to reproduce. It is compiler-generated: `fact`,
`src/dungeon_ai_movement.c:88` is a plain
`CeilFixedPoint(pokemon_info->belly)` and the objdump of that same matching file
is exactly that idiom.

## What was hard

`SubFixedPoint` and `sub_02050EBC` were semantically right on the first draft and
both sat at **270 with 13 `r`-marked rows** — the fractional chain in the wrong
register. Neither declares any local, so the usual declaration-order lever had
nothing to permute. Swapping the two assignments **inside** the guard flipped the
colouring across the whole function: 270 → 0, one edit each.

Ruled out on the way, and worth a reviewer knowing: swapping the two *top-level*
subtractions stayed at 270 with a different 13 rows (MWCC re-sorts independent
halfword stores into address order regardless of source), and five of six
explicit-temporary spellings came back **byte-identical**.

## Open questions for a reviewer

- `sub_02050EBC` and `sub_02050F78` are unnamed here on purpose. If upstream
  wants them named, the arithmetic suggests an add and a min.
- `BinToDecFixedPoint` ignores `val->upper` entirely, so the conversion truncates
  outside the 16-bit integer range. That is faithful to retail, not a bug in the
  C, but it is worth a second opinion on whether the parameter should be
  `const`-qualified (measured: `const` is byte-neutral here).
