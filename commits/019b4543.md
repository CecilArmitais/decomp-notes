**Fact.** All five follow `DseTrackEvent_SetTuning` in
`lib/DSE/src/main_02071BF4.c`, already landed and a complete template for this
shape: write the fade, recompute the final value, then mask interrupts through
`IME` while walking `channel->voice_list` and setting an `update_flags` bit on
every voice.

**Fact.** The bit differs by what changed -- `0x20` for volume and expression,
`0x40` for pan, where `SetTuning` uses `0x10`.

## Offsets resolve against existing types

**Fact.** `struct dse_fade` is `0x10`, so the channel's bend, volume and pan
fades sit at `0x1c`, `0x2c` and `0x3c` -- which is what the stores at
`0x2c`/`0x34`/`0x38` and `0x3c`/`0x44`/`0x48` address. `container` is at `0xc4`,
and `struct dse_synth` puts `pan` at `+7` and `song_and_global_volume` at `+8`,
both loaded with `ldrsb`.

**Fact.** The volume handlers scale by
`song_and_global_volume * volume * expression` and divide by `127 * 127`; the
compiler reproduces the target's `smull`-based division by that constant, so the
magic word `0x82061029` needs **no special handling**.

## Two findings that were not obvious

**1. Grouping decides instruction order.** The final pan is
`v + (container->pan - 0x40)`, **not** `(v + container->pan) - 0x40`. The target
subtracts before adding; the other grouping emits the two instructions in the
opposite order.

**2. A discarded read only survives if the type is `volatile`.** The read of
`IME` before restoring it is discarded, and **without `volatile` the compiler
drops the load entirely**, leaving only the restore.

That second one generalises into a diagnostic rule now in
`docs/MATCHING_TIPS.md`: *a load count that differs by one is a `volatile`
question, not a logic one* -- in both directions (a missing load wants
`volatile`; an extra one wants a non-volatile view).
