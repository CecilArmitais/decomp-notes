**`GetWindowContents` is the function the menu accessors landed over the previous
eight commits all call.** It reads the pointer at `0xC` of a `WINDOW_LIST` entry,
and its callers cast that to whichever menu layout they use -- which is what
justifies the two independent views introduced in
[`f7fbcd80`](f7fbcd80.md).

**Fact.** `struct Window` gains the fields these touch: bytes at `0x4`, `0x5`,
`0x7` and `0x8` around the existing `width` at `0x6`, and the pointer at `0xC`.
The trailing padding shrinks to keep the entry at `0xE0`.

**Fact.** `GetWindowRectangle` converts four of those bytes into a rectangle in
pixels, multiplying each by 8, writing top and bottom from `0x5` and `0x7` and
left and right from `0x4` and `width`. Its out parameter has no existing type and
becomes `struct unk_02028284`.

## The finding: bind the entry address to a pointer local

**Fact.** `GetWindowContents` needed the entry address bound to a pointer local.
Returning `WINDOW_LIST[window_id].field_0xC` **folds the field offset into the
literal pool entry**, giving `.word WINDOW_LIST+0xc` and an indexed load; the
target keeps `WINDOW_LIST` as the literal and reaches the field with `mla` plus
`ldr [r1, #0xc]`.

**This is the exact inverse of what five functions need in
[`7f6977e2`](7f6977e2.md)**, where the target *does* fold. The two together are
what turned this into a stated rule: **if the target's pool has the bare base
symbol, take the pointer local; if it has the folded address, index directly.**
